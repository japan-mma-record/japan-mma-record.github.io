Competitors can earn a spot by winning ADCC trials or by invitation.{{cite web |last1=de Souza |first1=Diogo |title=How Do You Earn An Invite To ADCC? |url=https://jitsmagazine.com/how-do-you-earn-an-invite-to-adcc/ |website=Jitsmagazine |access-date=16 May 2024}} == History == The competition was created by Sheik [[Tahnoun bin Zayed Al Nahyan (national security advisor)|Tahnoun Bin Zayed Al Nahyan]], the son of the former [[United Arab Emirates]] president [[Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan|Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan]], together with his [[Brazilian jiu-jitsu]] instructor Nelson Monteiro.{{cite web|url=https://www.espn.com/blog/mma/post/_/id/959/prince-of-a-guy-qatari-royalty-to-fight|title=Prince of a guy: Qatari royalty to fight|work=ESPN.com|date=27 May 2009}}{{cite web|url=http://adcombat.com/abu-dhabi-combat-club|title=Abu Dhabi Combat Club|work=adcombat.com}} As a young man, the future Sheikh was studying in the United States when he watched [[UFC 1]] and saw [[Royce Gracie]] win the tournament using Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu. Inspired by him, Al Nahyan learned the martial art.{{Cite web |date=2017-05-22 |title=Submission grappling: uma breve história do Jiu-Jitsu sem kimono |url=https://sensobjj.com/blogs/graciemag-1/submission-grappling-uma-breve-historia-do-jiu-jitsu-sem-kimono |access-date=2026-09-08 |website=Sensō Jiu Jitsu}} In 1997, Sheik Al Nahyan together with Nelson Monteiro promoted the ''Pentagon Combat'', a [[Vale Tudo]] event in [[Rio de Janeiro]]. The event was intended to serve as the innaugural event to a Brazilian counterpart to the [[Ultimate Fighting Championship]] (UFC), where Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu fighters would test their skills against practitioners of other martial arts in full-contact combat. The main event featured Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu practitioner [[Renzo Gracie]] against [[Luta Livre]] fighter [[Eugenio Tadeu|Eugênio Tadeu]], and was seen as another chapter in the longstanding rivalry between Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu and [[Luta Livre]]. The bout ended in a no contest after an altercation between the fighters' supporters escalated into a full riot, bringing the event to an abrupt end.{{Cite web |date=2016-11-16 |title=A história do MMA e vale-tudo pré-boom do UFC {{!}} Graciemag |url=https://www.graciemag.com/a-historia-do-mma-e-do-vale-tudo-antes-do-boom-do-ufc/ |access-date=2026-09-08 |language=pt-BR}} The incident contributed to the banning of Vale Tudo events in Rio de Janeiro and led Sheik Al Nahyan to reconsider the format of his planned combat sports competition. Rather than continuing with [[Vale Tudo]], he and Monteiro developed the idea of what was essentially a "UFC without punches": a competition in which practitioners of different grappling and submission arts could face one another under a common set of rules, but without strikes. In keeping with the original concept of the UFC, which had pitted practitioners of different martial arts against one another, the new competition would bring together athletes from Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu, [[Luta Livre]], [[wrestling]], [[judo]], [[Sambo (martial art)|sambo]], [[catch-as-catch-can]], and other grappling disciplines to determine which styles were most effective in [[submission fighting]]. This concept became the Abu Dhabi Combat Club Submission Fighting World Championship, with the inaugural event held in Abu Dhabi in March 1998. [[Royler Gracie]] characterised this as the beginning of "the modern era of submission grappling".{{Citation |first=Royler |last=Gracie |author-link=Royler Gracie |publisher=Invisible Cities Press |year=2003 |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=zifrv0mqvCoC&q=adcc+abu+dhabi |title=Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu Submission Grappling Techniques|isbn=978-1-931229-29-6 }} Thousands of [[submission grappling]] tournaments around the world use elements of the ADCC format and rule structure. To date, the great majority of ADCC champions have been [[Black belt (martial arts)|black belts]] in [[Brazilian jiu-jitsu]], which has the most similar rules to ADCC.{{Citation |title=The MMA Encyclopedia |first=Jonathan |last=Snowden |author2-first=Kendall |author2-last=Shields |year=2010 |publisher=[[ECW Press]] |isbn=978-1-55490-844-8 |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=lzdXP08P1pkC&pg=PT27}} However, there have been notable exceptions to this such as Americans [[Mark Kerr (fighter)|Mark Kerr]] ([[Collegiate wrestling|collegiate]] and [[freestyle wrestling]]) and [[Jeff Monson]] (collegiate and freestyle wrestling), Russian [[Karimula Barkalaev]] ([[Sambo (martial art)|sambo]], judo and MMA), Japanese [[Sanae Kikuta]] ([[judo]]/[[catch wrestling]]), and South African [[Mark Robinson (martial artist)|Mark Robinson]] (judo, freestyle, [[Greco-Roman wrestling|Greco-Roman]], and [[sumo]] wrestling), along with [[Silver medal|silver]] and [[Bronze medal|bronze medalists]] such as [[Caol Uno]] ([[Shooto]]/catch wrestling), [[Hayato Sakurai|Mach Sakurai]] (catch wrestling/judo), [[Aleksandr Savko]] (freestyle wrestling), [[Tito Ortiz]] (collegiate wrestling and MMA), [[Megumi Fujii]] (judo/[[Sambo (martial art)|sambo]]), [[Seiko Yamamoto]] (freestyle wrestling) and [[Nick Rodriguez]] (collegiate wrestling). === 2019 onwards === Mo Jassim took over as head organizer ready for ADCC 2019 and has since made sweeping changes to the organization.
