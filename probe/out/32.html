The event was broadcast live on [[pay-per-view]] and later released on [[home video]].{{cite news|title= Television: Pay-Per-View Battle, Instead of Being Merely Gory and Funny, Gets Interesting After the First Two Bouts|newspaper=[[The Los Angeles Times]]|date=November 15, 1993 |url= https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1993-11-15-ca-57200-story.html|access-date=July 1, 2011 |last=Rosenberg |first=Howard}}{{cite web|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=FtADAAAAMBAJ&q=shotokan+taekwondo+and+kung+fu+challenge+jujutsu+sandra+e+kessler&pg=PA24|title=Shotokan, Taekwondo, and Kung Fu challenge Jujutsu|last=Sandra E. Kessler|date=1 April 1994|website=[[Black Belt Magazine]]|publisher=[[Active Interest Media]], Inc.|via=[[Google Books]]|access-date=6 September 2017}}\n\nThe event was the lowest profile by the contemporary standards. The venue was less than half-packed, and the grand prize of the tournament was as big as a regular [[sparring]] partner's biannual salary. Major martial-arts observers and columnists did not bother to show up; the press neglected the event, and ''[[Black Belt Magazine|Black Belt]]'' mentioned it only several months later. Big-name fighters turned down the offers to participate or to make a [[guest appearance]] in the audience. \n\nThe event pioneered the interstylistic match-ups between the practitioners of different martial arts, and set the pattern for the future [[sporting events]] of the kind. UFC 1 would also be the first ever martial-arts event to use the [[octagon (mixed martial arts)|octagon]].\n\n==Background==\n''UFC 1'' was co-created by [[Rorion Gracie]] and the [[Torrance, California|Torrance]]-based UFC promoter [[Art Davie]], who decided to take locally famous [[Gracie Challenge#Various challenges|Gracie Garage Challenge]] fights versus California's martial artists to a new level, televised nationally, with the opponents picked internationally.\n\nThey did not come up with a [[Round of 16|16-man]] tournament, as the big-name martial artists, mainly kickboxers, namely [[Dennis Alexio]], [[Benny Urquidez]], [[Jean-Yves Thériault (kickboxer)|Jean-Yves Thériault]], [[Rick Roufus]], [[Stan Longinidis]], [[Maurice Smith (kickboxer)|Maurice Smith]], [[Bart Vale]], [[Hee Il Cho]], [[George Dillman]], [[Gene LeBell]], [[Rob Kaman]], [[Peter Aerts]], [[Ernesto Hoost]], [[Masaaki Satake]], were among the others \"publicly invited\" by Art Davie,{{cite journal|title=Promoter Issues Invitation|journal=Black Belt|date=November 1994|volume=30|number=11|pages=6–7}} but had shown no interest in participating.{{cite journal|date=August 1994|title=Still King of the Hill: Jujutsu Fighter Royce Gracie Tells the World \"If You Want Me, Come Get Me!\" (An Interview by Sandra E. Kessler)|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=QNADAAAAMBAJ|journal=Black Belt|volume=32|pages=48–52|number=8}} Davie placed advertisements in martial arts magazines to recruit fighters. He found less than a dozen who answered the call.{{cite book|last=Merlino|first=Doug|title=Beast: Blood, Struggle, and Dreams at the Heart of Mixed Martial Arts|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=rOCHCgAAQBAJ|year=2015|publisher=Bloomsbury Publishing USA |isbn=9781620401569}} The promoters came up with an eight-man [[tournament]] format, with the winner receiving $50,000.{{citation needed|date=September 2024}}\n\nThey wanted it to look brutal on television, so [[John Milius]], one of Rorion Gracie's students and a Hollywood veteran who had directed ''[[Conan the Barbarian (1982 film)|Conan the Barbarian]]'', decided the fights should take place in an [[octagonal]] cage [[Chain-link fencing|fenced with chain link]]. [[Campbell McLaren]], a SEG executive, wanted people to consider the championship a live, televised version of ''[[Mortal Kombat (1992 video game)|Mortal Kombat]]'', a popular [[fighting video game]], in which victorious fighters got to [[fatality (Mortal Kombat)|\"finish\" their opponents]] through moves such as [[Sub-Zero (Mortal Kombat)|ripping their spines out]] of their bodies. That one and the Davie's idea to top the cage with [[razor wire]] were rejected. UFC promoters initially pitched the event as a real-life fighting video game tournament similar to ''Mortal Kombat'' and ''[[Street Fighter]]''.{{cite web |last1=Snowden |first1=Jonathan |title=UFC 1, 25 Years Later: The Story Behind the Event That Started an Industry |url=https://bleacherreport.com/articles/2804552-ufc-1-25-years-later-the-story-behind-the-event-that-started-an-industry |website=[[Bleacher Report]] |access-date=5 November 2020 |date=12 November 2018}}\n\nGeneral regulations agreed upon were:\n\n*No [[out of competition drugs test|doping probes]].\n*[[wikt:no holds barred|No holds barred]].\n*No [[biting]].\n*No [[eye-gouging]]. \n*No mandatory [[MMA gloves|gloves]] and combative uniform (bare-knuckle contest).\n*No [[decisions in combat sports|judges' scores]].\n*Unlimited five-minute rounds with one-minute rest period in between. (Changed to no time limits for UFC 2 since no UFC 1 fight lasted five minutes.){{Cite web|url=https://www.bloodyelbow.com/2012/12/22/3712764/mma-ufc-origins-history-rules-regulation-fight-survival-legal-court-lawsuit-|title=MMA Origins: The UFC's Fight for Survival|date=22 December 2012|access-date=17 March 2021|archive-date=28 November 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211128153658/https://www.bloodyelbow.com/2012/12/22/3712764/mma-ufc-origins-history-rules-regulation-fight-survival-legal-court-lawsuit-|url-status=dead}}Joe Rogan Experience MMA Show #26 with Big John McCarthy\n*[[Knockout]], [[submission (combat sports)|tapout]], or [[corner stoppage]] (indicated by towel) are the only determination methods. Referee could only halt a match pending the corner decision.\n\nMcNichols Sports Arena in Denver, at an [[elevation]] [[Height above mean sea level|above mean sea level]] of approximately {{convert|1|mile|spell=in}}, had been chosen because Colorado had no athletic commission and thus no governing body from which they would need to get approval for bare-knuckle fighting. The arena had hosted only two fight [[card (sports)|cards]] in its history, both of minor significance, occurring earlier in 1993.[https://boxrec.com/en/location/31507 McNichols Sports Arena information] at the Boxing's Official Record Keeper, ''BoxRec.com''.\n\nThe major accomplishment though for the promoters was to gather a celebrity commentary team for the event. The commentary team for the pay-per-view was [[Bill Wallace (martial arts)|Bill Wallace]], [[Jim Brown]], and [[Kathy Long]], with additional analysis from [[Rod Machado]] and post-fight interviews by [[Brian Kilmeade]]. The ring announcer was Rich Goins.\n\n[[Jason DeLucia]] was an alternate for the event, having defeated [[Trent Jenkins]] in the alternate bout. However, as no fighter pulled out during the tournament, he was not called upon.\n\n==History==\nThe tournament featured fights with no weight classes, rounds, or judges. The three rules – no biting, no eye gouging, and no groin shots – were to be enforced only by a $1,500 fine. The match only ended by [[submission (combat sport term)|submission]], [[knockout]], or the fighter's corner [[Throw in the towel|throwing in the towel]], although the referee stopped the first fight at 26 seconds. Gloves were allowed, as [[Art Jimmerson]] showed in his quarterfinal bout against [[Royce Gracie]], which he fought with one boxing glove.\n\nRoyce Gracie won the tournament by defeating [[Gerard Gordeau]] via submission due to a [[rear naked choke]].{{cite news|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/mixed-martial-arts/46015665|title=UFC 1: The Beginning: Playboy, Mortal Kombat and the hunt for an ultimate fighter|work=BBC Sport|date=9 November 2018|access-date=13 November 2018}} The [[referees]] for ''UFC 1'' were João Alberto Barreto and Hélio Vigio, two veteran [[vale tudo]] referees from Brazil.\n\n==Results==\n{{MMAevent}}\n{{MMAevent card|Final}}\n{{MMAevent bout|N/A|[[Royce Gracie]]|def.|[[Gerard Gordeau]]|Submission (rear-naked choke)||1:44|\n}}\n{{MMAevent card|Alternate bout|header=no}}\n{{MMAevent bout|N/A|[[Jason DeLucia]]|def.|[[Trent Jenkins]]|Submission (rear-naked choke)||0:52|\n}}\n{{MMAevent card|Semi-finals|header=no}}\n{{MMAevent bout|N/A|[[Royce Gracie]]|def.|[[Ken Shamrock]]|Submission (rear-naked choke)||0:57|\n}}\n{{MMAevent bout|N/A|[[Gerard Gordeau]]|def.|[[Kevin Rosier]]|TKO (punches)||0:59|\n}}\n{{MMAevent card|Quarter-finals|header=no}}\n{{MMAevent bout|N/A|[[Ken Shamrock]]|def.|[[Patrick Smith (fighter)|Patrick Smith]]|Submission (heel hook)||1:49|\n}}\n{{MMAevent bout|N/A|[[Royce Gracie]]|def.|[[Art Jimmerson]]|Submission (smother choke)||2:18|\n}}\n{{MMAevent bout|N/A|[[Kevin Rosier]]|def.|[[Zane Frazier]]|TKO (corner stoppage)||4:20|\n}}\n{{MMAevent bout|N/A|[[Gerard Gordeau]]|def.|[[Teila Tuli]]|TKO (head kick)||0:26|\n}}\n{{MMAevent end|notes = yes}}\n\n==''UFC 1'' bracket==\n{{Round8\n\n\n||{{flagicon|NED}} '''[[Gerard Gordeau]]'''

The event was seen live on pay-per-view in the [[United States]], and later released on [[home video]].\n\n==History==\nThe event featured a four-man [[Middleweight (MMA)|Middleweight]] Tournament, three [[Heavyweight (MMA)|Heavyweight]] Superfights, an alternate bout in case of tournament injury, and a [[Heavyweight (MMA)|Heavyweight]] \"exhibition\" bout. A [[UFC Light Heavyweight Championship]] bout between current champion [[Frank Shamrock]] and [[Jeremy Horn]] was also taped for broadcast on a later pay-per-view.\n\nUFC 17 was the last [[Ultimate Fighting Championship|UFC]] event (aside from [[UFC 23]]), to feature the \"tournament\" style format.\n\n\nSeveral hours prior to the event, Olympic Gold medalist and [[Ultimate Fighting Championship|UFC]] commentator [[Jeff Blatnick]] was introduced as the promotion's commissioner in a meeting with the fighters to discuss the fight rules for the event. Blatnick asked at the meeting that the fighters refer to the sport, which at the time had many different names including the misnomer \"No Holds Barred\", as ''[[Mixed Martial Arts]]'', with the intention of improving the sport's image. He then repeated the request to the media covering the event later that night. This is generally considered the first time the term [[Mixed martial arts|Mixed Martial Arts]] was used to refer to the modern sport, and Blatnick is widely credited with coining the term in relation to it.{{cite web|url=http://www.mmafighting.com/2012/10/27/3560844/blatnicks-funeral-and-how-the-term-mma-came-to-be|title=Jeff Blatnick's funeral and how the term MMA came to be|work=MMAFighting.com|author=Meltzer, Dave|date=October 27, 2012}}\n\nUFC 17 featured the first [[Ultimate Fighting Championship|UFC]] appearance of both [[Dan Henderson]] and [[Carlos Newton]], as well as the first [[Mixed martial arts|MMA]] fight for [[Chuck Liddell]].\n\nThe title ''Redemption'' refers to [[Mark Coleman]]'s return after losing the [[UFC Heavyweight Championship]] to [[Maurice Smith (fighter)|Maurice Smith]] at [[UFC 14]]. Coleman was originally slated to face then [[UFC Heavyweight Championship|UFC Heavyweight Champion]] [[Randy Couture]], who had to withdraw due to an injury. [[Lion's Den (mixed martial arts)|Lion's Den]] fighter [[Pete Williams (fighter)|Pete Williams]] would step in as a last-minute replacement to face Coleman. Similar to his fight with Maurice, Coleman looked good early on in the fight, but when the fight went into an overtime round, Coleman appeared to be drained of energy, breathing heavily and keeping his arms low—even turning his back to Williams occasionally while standing. He took several hard shots from Williams, and was eventually knocked out by a devastating head kick Williams delivered to the face. The event was seen on [[pay-per-view]] in the [[United States]] and [[Brazil]], and was later released on [[home video]].\n\n==History==\n''Ultimate Brazil'' was the first appearance of the UFC in Brazil, and the second UFC event to take place outside the US. The event was arranged through the initiative of Sérgio Batarelli, former [[Kickboxing]] and [[Vale Tudo]] fighter, promoter of the [[International Vale Tudo Championship]], WVC (World Vale Tudo Championship) and manager of multiple Brazilian MMA fighters.{{Cite web|first=Alexandre|last=Fernandes|title=Sergio Batarelli: 'O MMA de hoje é brincadeira perto do que era o IVC'|url=http://sportv.globo.com/site/combate/noticia/2013/12/sergio-batarelli-o-mma-de-hoje-e-brincadeira-perto-do-que-era-o-ivc.html|date=2013-12-23|language=pt-BR|access-date=2022-01-10|website=sportv.globo.com/site/combate}} ''Ultimate Brazil'' marked the first UFC event (other than [[UFC 9]]) to not utilize the tournament format which, aside from [[UFC 23]], was abandoned completely in following events. The event featured the first ever UFC Welterweight Championship fight, as well as a Middleweight Championship fight.\n\nUltimate Brazil was part one of what the UFC called \"The Road To The Heavyweight Title\" (not to be confused with the [[UFC 18|event of the same name]]), a tournament of sorts, spanning four events, to crown the new UFC Heavyweight Champion following [[Randy Couture]]'s relinquishing of the belt.\n\nUFC Brazil marked the first appearance of [[Pedro Rizzo]], who would go on to become a top UFC heavyweight contender. The event also featured the first UFC appearance of future [[Pride Fighting Championships|PRIDE]] [[List_of_Pride_Fighting_Championships_champions#Middleweight_Championship|Middleweight Champion]] [[Wanderlei Silva]] in a fight against former [[UFC 12]] heavyweight tournament champion and future [[List of UFC champions#Light Heavyweight Championship|UFC Light Heavyweight Champion]] [[Vitor Belfort]].
