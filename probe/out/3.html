Originally established as a regional promotion, the promotion was acquired by [[Zuffa]], then the parent company of the [[Ultimate Fighting Championship]] (UFC), in 2006. Under Zuffa's ownership, the WEC underwent a gradual transformation, eventually focusing primarily on the lighter weight classes and developing a roster that included many fighters who would later become prominent UFC competitors.\n\nIn its final years, the WEC contested bouts in three weight classes: [[Bantamweight (MMA)|bantamweight]] (135 lb; 61 kg), featherweight (145 lb; 66 kg), and lightweight (155 lb; 70 kg). The promotion was also distinguished by its use of a 25-foot (7.6 m) diameter cage, smaller than the standard 30-foot (9.1 m) UFC cage. The WEC held its final event in December 2010, after which its remaining divisions and fighters were incorporated into the UFC.\n\n==History==\nScott Adams and Reed Harris started the organization in 2001. Between 2001 and 2006, most of their events were held at [[Tachi Palace Hotel & Casino]] in [[Lemoore, California]], and aired on [[HDNet]].\nIn December 2006, Zuffa purchased WEC. With the purchase, WEC continued as a separate promotion with its own roster of fighters. Adams was retained after the purchase as the organization's matchmaker. Harris and Adams were named co-General Managers and were both active in the new WEC.{{cite web\n| url=http://www.mmaweekly.com/absolutenm/templates/dailynews.asp?articleid=3053\n| title=UFC Buying World Extreme Cagefighting\n| author=Ken Pishna, Ivan Trembow\n| date=December 11, 2006\n| publisher= MMAWeekly\n}}\n In 2008, Sean Shelby was named the new match-maker for the promotion by Zuffa.\n\nZuffa made several changes to the promotion after the purchase. It discontinued its pentagonal cage for a modified version of the UFC's octagonal cage, with a 25-foot diameter instead of the UFC's 30-foot diameter cage. The championships of fighters who were contracted UFC fighters were vacated. The promotion moved to focus on lighter weight classes, abandoning their [[Heavyweight (MMA)|heavyweight]] and [[Super Heavyweight (MMA)|super heavyweight]] divisions and championships, and retaining their [[Bantamweight (MMA)|bantamweight]] and [[Featherweight (MMA)|featherweight]] divisions—two divisions not then present in the UFC.{{cite web\n| url=http://www.sherdog.com/news/articles.asp?n_id=6478\n| title=The Savage Truth: When is a Loss a Win?\n| last=Savage\n| first=Greg\n| date=January 4, 2007\n| publisher=[[Sherdog]]\n}}\n After the purchase, several WEC events were held in Zuffa's hometown of [[Las Vegas, Nevada]], much like the [[Ultimate Fighting Championship|UFC]].\n\nWEC aired their events live on Versus (formerly known as the Outdoor Life Network and now as the [[NBC Sports Network]]) in the United States and on [[The Sports Network|TSN]] (and later [[The Score Television Network|The Score]]) in Canada.{{cite web\n| url=http://www.mmaweekly.com/absolutenm/templates/dailynews.asp?articleid=3186&zoneid=13\n| title=UFC on HBO; WEC on Versus\n| last=Pishna\n| first=Ken\n| date=December 28, 2006\n| publisher= MMAWeekly\n}}\n WEC's first live event was broadcast on June 3, 2007, on Versus from [[The Joint (music venue)|The Joint]] at the [[Hard Rock Hotel and Casino (Las Vegas)|Hard Rock Hotel and Casino]] in Las Vegas.\n\n[[Todd Harris]] was the play by play announcer on every televised WEC event on Versus, and was joined by former UFC Heavyweight Champion [[Frank Mir]] on color commentary until the spring of 2010, when Mir was replaced by UFC light heavyweight [[Stephan Bonnar]]. Fellow Zuffa fighters [[Kenny Florian]] and [[Jens Pulver]] have also filled in on color commentary due to Mir or Bonnar's absences. UFC announcers [[Mike Goldberg]] and [[Joe Rogan]] called WEC's only pay-per-view event, [[WEC 48]] in April 2010. Postfight interviews on the Versus-televised cards were typically done by either Harris or Versus sportscaster [[Craig Hummer]].\n\n[[Joe A. Martinez|Joe Martinez]] served as the announcer for Zuffa-promoted WEC events until April 2010, when he left the organization and was replaced by UFC announcer [[Bruce Buffer]]. However, Martinez made a one-off return at [[WEC 52]] that November when Buffer was overseas for [[UFC 122|a UFC event]].\n\nWEC announced that it would dissolve their light heavyweight and middleweight divisions after their December 3, 2008 event to further their concentration on lighter classes. Fighters from the light heavyweight and middleweight divisions started fighting in the UFC after the dissolution of their divisions.{{cite news|url=http://www.mmaweekly.com/absolutenm/templates/dailynews.asp?articleid=7206&zoneid=2|title=WEC shuts down 205 & 185-pound weight classes |first=Damon |last=Martin |work=MMAWeekly.com |date=August 26, 2008 |access-date=August 26, 2008}} Also, on February 3, 2009, WEC officially announced the creation of a {{convert|125|lb|kg|abbr=on}} [[Flyweight (MMA)|flyweight]] division and the dissolution of its welterweight division (flyweight would have been the last division under the \"[[Mixed martial arts rules#Unified Rules of Mixed Martial Arts|Unified Rules of MMA]]\" to be activated under Zuffa, excluding [[Super heavyweight (MMA)|Super Heavyweight]]). Fighters from the welterweight division started fighting in the UFC after the dissolution of their division. The only weight class that still crossed over between WEC and the UFC prior to the merger was the {{convert|155|lb|kg|abbr=on}} lightweight division.{{cite news |date=February 3, 2009 |access-date=May 1, 2009 |url=http://www.mmaweekly.com/absolutenm/templates/dailynews.asp?articleid=8115&zoneid=13 |title=WEC axes welterweights; announces flyweights |publisher=MMAWeekly.com}}\n\nOn January 8, 2010, World Extreme Cagefighting announced that [[Amp Energy]] would be the official energy drink of WEC. Amp Energy also sponsored three of WEC's top stars - [[Featherweight (MMA)|featherweight]] [[Urijah Faber]], featherweight [[Chad Mendes]], and [[Bantamweight (MMA)|bantamweight]] [[Joseph Benavidez]].\n\nOn April 24, 2010, WEC held its only [[pay-per-view]] event, [[WEC 48]], which featured two championship fights. The main event featured WEC Featherweight Champion [[José Aldo]] defending his title against former champion [[Urijah Faber]].{{cite web|url=http://www.wec.tv/index.cfm?fa=eventdetail.fightCard&eid=2922|title=WEC|website=www.wec.tv}}\n\nOn October 28, 2010, UFC President [[Dana White]] announced that WEC would merge with the [[Ultimate Fighting Championship|UFC]].{{cite web |url=http://mmajunkie.com/news/21189/ufc-and-wec-set-to-merge-in-2011-events-to-air-on-versus-and-spike-tv.mma |title=UFC and WEC set to merge in 2011; events to air on Versus and Spike TV {{!}} MMAjunkie.com |website=mmajunkie.com |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101030032837/http://mmajunkie.com/news/21189/ufc-and-wec-set-to-merge-in-2011-events-to-air-on-versus-and-spike-tv.mma |archive-date=2010-10-30}} \n\n==Relationship with the UFC==\nDue to its shared ownership under Zuffa, fans and commentators had continually speculated about a unification between WEC and the UFC, particularly the lower weight classes.{{cite web|url=https://www.usatoday.com/sports/mma/2008-07-30-wec_N.htm|title=UFC has weight-and-see attitude for WEC - USATODAY.com|website=www.usatoday.com}}{{cite web|url=http://sportsillustrated.cnn.com/2009/mma/08/25/ufc.wec.merger/index.html|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100112162524/http://sportsillustrated.cnn.com/2009/mma/08/25/ufc.wec.merger/index.html|url-status=dead|archive-date=January 12, 2010|title=A UFC-WEC merger? Following the purchase of WEC, Zuffa made several changes to the promotion. One of the most noteworthy changes included added media exposure when WEC signed a deal to air events on [[Versus (TV channel)|Versus]] beginning with [[WEC 28]]. Prior to this WEC had broadcast most of their events on [[HDNet]].\n\nWhile on [[Versus (TV channel)|Versus]], most events aired mid-week, or on Sunday nights during the summer months. WEC drew good ratings that averaged around 575,000 average viewers per event. However ratings ranged from 245,000 to 1,500,000.\n\nThe biggest ratings draw for WEC was [[Urijah Faber]]. When Faber was featured in the main event he drew an average of 840,000 viewers.
