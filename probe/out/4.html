Cage Warriors was established in 2001 and staged its first [[Mixed Martial Arts|MMA]] event in London in July, 2002. Currently it has scheduled events in [[Chechnya]], [[Baghdad]] and [[Beirut]].\n\nMany of the biggest stars in MMA have made their names under the Cage Warriors banner, including [[Conor McGregor]] and [[Michael Bisping]]. CWFC are one of the few top promotions to take their events abroad, travelling throughout countries in [[Europe]], [[North America]] and the [[Middle East]].{{cite web|last=Bower |first=Mick |url=http://www.sabotagetimes.com/football-sport/mmas-arab-spring-how-cage-warriors-are-taking-over-the-middle-east/ |title=MMA's Arab Spring: How Cage Warriors Are Taking Over The Middle East |publisher=Sabotage Times |date=2012-05-25 |accessdate=2014-02-10}} CWFC staged 26 events across 12 different countries in 2012 and 2013.\n\n==History==\n\n\nCage Warriors was originally founded by Dougie Truman back in 2001, the first event titled 'Armageddon', at which notable fighters like Jean Silva, [[Rosi Sexton]], Matt Ewin and Paul Jenkins were victorious,{{cite web|url=http://www.sherdog.com/events/CWFC-1-Armaggeddon-1078 |title=CWFC 1 - Armageddon |publisher=Sherdog.com |date=2002-07-27 |accessdate=2014-02-10}} was staged on July 27 of that year.\n\nIrishman Graham Boylan assumed control of Cage Warriors in 2010 and has successfully set about returning the promotion to the forefront of MMA in Europe, as well as expanding internationally with shows in [[Ireland]], [[Russia]], [[Ukraine]], [[Jordan]], [[United Arab Emirates]], [[Lebanon]] and [[Bahrain]]. Ian Dean is Cage Warriors' well-known matchmaker, while Paul Dollery is the promotion's Director of Media and Operations. The Cage Warriors head office is located in [[London]].{{cite web|url=http://www.bloodyelbow.com/2015/2/11/8017991/cage-warriors-ceo-graham-boylan-steps-down-mma-news |title=Cage Warriors CEO resigns, promotion's future uncertain |publisher=Bloodyelbow.com |date=2015-02-11 |accessdate=2015-07-01}}{{cite web|url=http://www.mmafighting.com/2015/6/29/8862797/graham-boylan-acquires-cage-warriors-brand-eyes-potential-rebrand-and |title=Graham Boylan acquires Cage Warriors brand, eyes potential rebrand and two shows in 2015 |publisher=Mmafighting.com |date= |accessdate=2015-07-01}}\n\nOn October 25, 2012, it was announced that, along with [[Ultimate Challenge MMA]] and [[BAMMA]], Cage Warriors have united with The Centre of Health and Human Performance in London to launch SAFE MMA, a non-profit organization established to improve the safety of MMA fighters in the U.K. Chief among the organization's plans is a centralized and confidential database for fighter medical records. Nevada State Athletic Commission. Retrieved 4 October 2006 These rules have been adopted across the US in other states that regulate mixed martial arts. As a result, they have become the standard ''de facto'' set of rules for professional mixed martial arts across the US and for cage-based MMA worldwide.\n\nAll Cage Warriors fights are contested over three, five-minute rounds, with the exception of five-round championship bouts. There is a one-minute rest period in-between rounds. As per the Unified Rules of MMA, Cage Warriors only allows competitors to fight in approved shorts, without shoes or any other sort of foot padding. Fighters must use approved light gloves (4-6 ounces) that allow fingers to grab. The referee has the right to stop the fighters and stand them up if they reach a stalemate on the ground (where neither are in a dominant [[Grappling position|position]] nor working toward one) after a verbal warning.\n\n===Match outcome===\nMatches usually end via:\n*'''[[Submission (combat sport term)|Submission]]''': a fighter taps on the mat or his opponent three times (or more) or verbally submits.\n*'''[[Knockout]]''': a fighter falls from a legal blow and is either unconscious or unable to immediately continue.\n*'''Technical Knockout''': stoppage of the fight by the referee if it is determined a fighter cannot \"intelligently defend\" himself or by ringside doctor due to injury.\n*'''Judges' Decision''': Depending on scoring, a match may end as:\n**unanimous decision (all three judges score a win for one fighter),\n**split decision (two judges score a win for one fighter with the third for the other),\n**majority decision (two judges score a win for one fighter with one for a draw),\n**unanimous draw (all three judges score a draw),\n**majority draw (two judges score a draw).\n**split draw (the total points for each fighter is equal)\n\nA fight can also end in a '''technical decision''', '''technical draw''', '''disqualification''', '''forfeit''' or '''no contest'''.\n\n===Judging criteria===\nThe ten-point must system is used for all Cage Warriors fights; three judges score each round and the winner of each receives ten points, the loser nine points or less. If the round is even, both fighters receive ten points. The decision is announced at the end of the match but the judge's scorecards are not announced.\n\n===Fouls===\nThe following are considered fouls in Cage Warriors:\n\n# [[headbutt|Butting]] with the head.\n# [[eye-gouging|Eye gouging]] of any kind.\n# Biting.\n# Hair pulling.\n# [[fish-hooking|Fish hooking]].\n# [[Groin attack]]s of any kind.\n# Putting a finger into any orifice or into any cut or laceration on an opponent. (see [[Gouging (fighting style)|Gouging]])\n# [[Small joint manipulation]].\n# Striking to the spine or the back of the head. (see [[Rabbit punch]])\n# Striking downward using the point of the elbow. (see [[Elbow (strike)]])\n# Throat strikes of any kind, including, without limitation, grabbing the trachea.\n# Clawing, pinching or twisting the flesh.\n# Grabbing the [[clavicle]].\n# [[soccer kick|Kicking]] the head of a grounded opponent.\n# [[knee (strike)|Kneeing]] the head of a grounded opponent.\n# [[stomp (strike)|Stomping]] a grounded opponent.\n# [[Kick]]ing to the kidney with the heel.\n# Spiking an opponent to the canvas on his head or neck. (see [[piledriver (professional wrestling)]])\n# [[throw (grappling)|Throwing]] an opponent out of the ring or fenced area.\n# Holding the shorts or gloves of an opponent.\n# Spitting at an opponent.\n# Engaging in an unsportsmanlike conduct that causes an injury to an opponent.\n# Holding the ropes or the fence.\n# Using abusive language in the ring or fenced area.\n# Attacking an opponent on or during the break.\n# Attacking an opponent who is under the care of the referee.\n# Attacking an opponent after the bell has sounded the end of the period of unarmed combat.\n# Flagrantly disregarding the instructions of the referee.\n# Timidity, including, without limitation, avoiding contact with an opponent, intentionally or consistently dropping the mouthpiece or faking an injury.\n# Interference by the corner.\n# Throwing in the towel during competition.\n\nWhen a foul is charged, the referee in their discretion may deduct one or more points as a penalty. If a foul incapacitates a fighter, then the match may end in a disqualification if the foul was intentional, or a no contest if unintentional. If a foul causes a fighter to be unable to continue later in the bout, it ends with a technical decision win to the injured fighter if the injured fighter is ahead on points, otherwise it is a
